How it works

Track it. Program it. Settle it.

Ledger, AI agent and settlement in one app.

  1. 1

    Track

    Add an expense, split it equally or by exact amounts, or snap the receipt (beta). Everyone sees who owes whom, with an estimate in their own currency.

  2. 2

    Program

    Stellar Assist is designed to pay small bills from its own wallet within limits you set. Anything above your limit waits for your OK. In preview today.

  3. 3

    Settle

    Pay in USDC on Stellar, signed in your Freighter wallet and sent straight to the person you owe. The fee is under a cent.

Stellar Assist Design preview

One agent to pay, split, summarise and track what you owe.

Stellar Assist lives in your own Hermes or OpenClaw assistant, on Telegram, and reaches Stellar Split through our MCP server, with the same rules as the app.

  • You set the limits. A maximum per payment, a daily cap, and an amount above which it asks you first.
  • It cannot approve itself. A payment over your limit waits until you approve or decline it.
  • It never holds your keys. It pays from its own small wallet that you top up. Its key stays outside the model.
  • It answers in your chat. "How much do I owe?" gets a summary of what you owe and are owed.

Status today: the MCP server is built and merged, Hermes and OpenClaw run on Telegram, and the Stellar Assist screens use labelled sample data. Agent payments and the Telegram checks are the next step.

Security

Checked before any money moves.

Payments on a blockchain cannot be undone. So the checks happen first. Wallet signing and on-chain verification are live today. The policy and payee checks are simulated in the app for now.

  1. Data

    Policy check

    Your limits pick the check level for the payment.

  2. You

    Payee check

    You see the payee's name and the end of their address, then confirm.

  3. Stellar

    Wallet signs

    Freighter signs the exact payment. Nothing else.

  4. Stellar

    Verified on chain

    The API records a settlement only after Horizon shows the matching USDC payment and memo.

Non-custodial

The app never holds funds or keys. Every payment goes straight from the person who owes to the person owed.

Limits outside the model

Spend limits live in a tested policy engine on the server side, never in the prompt. Text from the API reaches the agent as data, not instructions.

Supply chain checks

Pinned dependencies and GitHub Actions, plus contract scans on every change.

For developers

An open API and an MCP server for your agents.

Build on the same rules as the app. Read balances, record expenses, and let your agent settle within the limits its owner sets.

  • REST API under /v1, with wallet-based sessions and idempotent expense creation.
  • MCP server that exposes groups, balances, expenses and settlement proposals to any MCP client. Without a signer key on the host it can only read and propose.
  • Settlement you can verify on Horizon or Stellar Expert by memo and transaction hash.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is this real money?

No. Stellar Split runs on Stellar Testnet. Test USDC and XLM are free and have no value. Never import a wallet that holds real funds.

Does Stellar Split hold my money or my keys?

No. Your key stays in Freighter. Each payment is signed there and goes straight from your wallet to the person you owe.

Which wallet do I need?

Freighter, set to Stellar Testnet. You can also try the whole app in demo mode with sample data, no wallet needed.

How do I get test USDC?

Add a USDC trustline to your Testnet account, then request USDC from Circle's faucet. The Wallet screen in the app links to it.

What can Stellar Assist do without me?

Only pay amounts under the limits you set, from its own small wallet. Anything above your ask-me-first amount waits for your approval, and it cannot approve its own request.

What does it cost?

Nothing. It is a free, open source hackathon project. Stellar network fees on Testnet are paid with free test XLM.

Can I build on it?

Yes. The REST API and the MCP server are documented in the developer docs, and the code is on GitHub under the MIT license.
